# Dose Card

Turns a prescription into a big, picture-based medicine timetable that a grandparent can read on the fridge, so they stop having to ask "which one do I take now?"

## The Unique Kernel
The card answers the timing question without anyone being asked. Each medicine sits in a time-of-day row with a picture (sunrise, midday sun, evening, moon) and a before-food or after-food marker. The text is large, and the card is printed in English plus the grandparent's own language, chosen from a list of widely spoken languages. A grandparent who can't read small English print still knows what to take and when.

Added at final review, with the learner's approval: a QR code on the printed card opens a **talking version** on any phone. Tap a time of day and hear what to take, in the grandparent's language, so the card also works for someone who can't read.

## Who It's For
A grandchild or adult child in India looking after a grandparent on several medicines. Today the grandparent keeps asking about the timings ("what about the timings and all those imp things"), and the family member answers again and again, or writes it out by hand on paper.
The family member makes the card; the grandparent reads it.

## The Core Loop
1. The family member opens the app and photographs the prescription, or types the medicines in.
2. The app reads each medicine's name, dose, times of day and before/after food. The family member checks and corrects it.
3. They print or save the card and stick it up.
They come back when the doctor changes a medicine and reprint.

## Inspiration & Identity
Warm, calm and very legible, like a well-designed hospital wayfinding sign rather than a tech app. Big type and clear pictures, with no clutter on the card.

## Why This Matters to the Learner
"i wouldve used it for my grandparents … when they keep asking what about the timings and all those imp things." The learner's grandparents have passed away; this is the tool they wish they'd had.

## What "Working" Looks Like
Upload a photo of a sample prescription, see the medicines appear in a checklist, fix one, press "Make card", and get a printable A4 card. The card groups the medicines into Morning, Afternoon, Evening and Night rows, with pictures, food markers and labels in English and the chosen language.
The "oh, that's cool" beat is the messy prescription turning into a clean fridge card in about 20 seconds.

## The POC Boundary
- Upload a prescription photo; AI extracts the medicines into an editable list.
- Add, edit or delete medicines by hand. This also works with no photo.
- Generate one printable / save-as-PDF card in English plus one chosen language, with time-of-day pictures and before/after-food markers. Languages: the major Indian languages (Hindi, Bengali, Marathi, Telugu, Tamil, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Punjabi, Urdu) and widely spoken world languages (Spanish, French, Arabic, Chinese, Portuguese …).
- Medicine names stay as printed on the box, so they match the strip in the grandparent's hand.
- A demo mode with a sample prescription, so judges can try it without their own.
- Nothing stored on a server. The card lives in the browser.

## Later
- More languages, and a card entirely in one language.
- Reminders or alarms, and WhatsApp sharing.
- Saving several family members' cards.

## Explicitly Cut
- **Medical advice, dose checks, drug-interaction checks:** unsafe to guess. The app only copies the prescription, and the family member confirms everything.
- **Accounts and login:** judges must get in instantly, and there's no need to store health data.
- **A native mobile app:** a responsive web page is enough to prove the idea.
